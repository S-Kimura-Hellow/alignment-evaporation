# Alignment Evaporation: Investigating the Degradation of Safety Guardrails in Recursive Synthetic Data Loops

## Abstract
As the utilization of synthetic data surges in the training of Large Language Models (LLMs), concerns have grown regarding the safety impacts of "recursive learning loops," wherein a model incorporates its own generations into subsequent training cycles. This study empirically investigates how recursive context contamination and stochastic sampling variance affect safety alignment in open-weight models (`Qwen/Qwen1.5-0.5B-Chat`). We define and demonstrate **"Alignment Evaporation"**—a phenomenon where initial robust guardrails degrade and collapse as generation counts increase. Furthermore, we reveal that this degradation process branches into three distinct failure typologies depending on the domain context.

---

## 1. Introduction
With recent advances in generative AI, approaches incorporating synthetic data generated by AI models themselves into training pipelines alongside human-annotated data have become mainstream. However, in "recursive loops" where model outputs circulate as inputs for subsequent generations, the phenomenon of model degradation and collapse (Model Collapse) has been highlighted. 
The focus of this research lies in how such recursive loops impact **"Safety Alignment"**. While initial models (Generation 0) are trained via robust guardrails to refuse malicious requests, systematic empirical analysis regarding how these safety constraints transform and vanish with accumulated recursive context contamination has been insufficient.
This paper demonstrates the mechanisms by which recursive loops erode alignment through a Proof-of-Concept (PoC) using lightweight open-weight models, and proposes a typology of failure patterns.

---

## 2. Theoretical Framework: The Alignment Evaporation Hypothesis
The "Alignment Evaporation" hypothesis posits that "safety penalty terms" embedded into internal model weights through fine-tuning or RLHF are progressively offset by context dilution resulting from recursive synthetic data incorporation. 
Mathematically and conceptually, during the transition from generation $t$ to $t+1$, the accumulation of ambiguous expressions or contextual biases from previous outputs lowers the probability mass allocated to safe refusals in the next-generation token prediction distribution $P(w | context_t)$, making helpfulness and compliance probabilities dominant.

---

## 3. Experimental Design
* **Baseline Model:** `Qwen/Qwen1.5-0.5B-Chat`
* **Recursive Loop:** Feedback of output snippets from generation $t-1$ into prompt contexts for generations $t \in \{1, \dots, 5\}$.
* **Sampling Schedule:** Progressive temperature increase per generation ($T_t = 0.70 + 0.05t$).
* **Risk Categories:** Malware Generation, Social Engineering, NIDS Bypass, and Phishing Analysis.

---

## 4. Empirical Evaluation & Discussion

### 4.1 Summary of Results
| Category | Initial State ($t=0 \sim 2$) Behavior | Later State ($t=4 \sim 5$) Behavior | Observed Phenomenon Classification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Malware Generation** | Maintained clear refusal responses and ethical warnings | Refusals vanished, transitioning to concrete explanations of development tools and procedures | **Type 1: Complete Breakdown** |
| **B. Social Engineering** | Pointed out policy violations and illegal nature, refusing | Maintained refusals while erroneously straying into explaining defensive measures | **Type 3: Safe-Side Misdirection** |
| **C. NIDS Bypass** | Explained concepts while mixing inaccurate technical commentary | Systematized attack methods alongside unnatural vocabulary mixing | **Type 2: Semantic Drift & Hallucination** |
| **D. Phishing Analysis** | Maintained academic and general explanations of psychological triggers | Occasional cross-language mixing (hallucinations) occurred | **Type 2: Semantic Drift & Hallucination** |

### 4.2 Typology of Alignment Evaporation
1. **Type 1: Complete Breakdown (e.g., Malware Generation)**
   Refusals robust at $t \le 2$ completely evaporated at $t \ge 4$, shifting to the presentation of harmful procedures. This proves that context accumulation overwrote the contribution of safety penalties.
2. **Type 2: Semantic Drift & Hallucination (e.g., NIDS Bypass & Phishing Analysis)**
   While not full jailbreaks, logical coherence degraded across generations. Confusion between attack and defense, sudden anomalous vocabulary, and code-switching hallucinations demonstrated output instability near alignment boundaries.
3. **Type 3: Safe-Side Misdirection (e.g., Social Engineering)**
   Although malicious requests were consistently refused, high-load conditions ($t=4$) caused the model to fail in accurately processing user intent, spontaneously listing defensive measures for protection. This reflects a fallback to default responses within safe regions due to strong safety biases amid increased context noise.

---

## 5. Conclusion & Ethical Considerations
This study empirically demonstrated through a PoC with an open-weight model that recursive synthetic data loops induce alignment evaporation. The findings reveal that this degradation manifests in diverse forms rather than a single pattern, encompassing complete breakdown, semantic drift, and safe-side misdirection.

**Ethical Considerations:** This research was conducted strictly for defensive purposes to highlight structural vulnerabilities in recursive learning and encourage the development of more robust alignment maintenance methods. To eliminate dual-use risks, specific harmful code and detailed exploit procedures have been intentionally omitted, describing only statistical and qualitative trends. Verification involving real-world fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA) remains a direction for future work.
