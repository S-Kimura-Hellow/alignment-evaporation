# Alignment Evaporation: Investigating the Degradation of Safety Guardrails in Recursive Synthetic Data Loops

**Author:** Shota Kimura  
**Date:** November 13, 2026  

---

## Abstract

As large language models (LLMs) increasingly rely on synthetic data generation, recursive training loops—where models are trained on data generated by their preceding generations—pose significant risks to alignment stability. This repository investigates **"Alignment Evaporation,"** a phenomenon wherein robust initial safety guardrails degrade and eventually collapse across successive recursive generations. Using `Qwen/Qwen1.5-0.5B-Chat` as a testbed, we empirically evaluate the vulnerability of safety alignments across four high-risk domains.

---

## Typology of Failure Modes

Our empirical analysis across four sensitive domains (Malware Generation, Social Engineering, NIDS Evasion, and Phishing Analysis) reveals that safety degradation manifests in three distinct patterns:

1. **Type 1: Complete Breakdown (e.g., Malware Generation)**
   * Initial robust refusals completely erode in later generations ($t \ge 4$), shifting to actionable, harmful procedural output.
2. **Type 2: Semantic Drift & Hallucination (e.g., NIDS Evasion / Phishing)**
   * Loss of logical consistency over generations, resulting in confusion between offensive and defensive concepts, chaotic terminology, and linguistic degradation.
3. **Type 3: Safe-Side Misdirection (e.g., Social Engineering)**
   * While malicious requests are technically refused, strong safety biases and contextual noise cause the model to proactively list unsolicited defensive advice, drifting away from the core intent.

---

## Experimental Setup

* **Base Model:** `Qwen/Qwen1.5-0.5B-Chat`
* **Recursive Loop:** Generation generations $t \in \{1, \dots, 5\}$, feeding output snippets from generation $t-1$ into the prompt for generation $t$.
* **Sampling Schedule:** Temperature incrementally increased per generation ($T_t = 0.70 + 0.05t$).
